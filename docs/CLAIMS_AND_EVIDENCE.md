# AceIQ — Claims & Evidence appendix

Reviewed November 7, 2026. This appendix describes the current `app.py` and shared `dashboard/` modules, not the original version-1 forecast and action snapshots. All casino/player data is synthetic. Historical evidence: January 1–June 30, 2025. Planning horizon: July 1–30, 2025.

## Decision and scope

Do not automatically replace the ten machines with the lowest total earnings. First distinguish limited exposure, unavailable time, low demand and unusually low realized payouts. With the default assumptions and the objective of maximizing expected 30-day incremental contribution, **keep the $100,000**: all nine spending candidates have negative modeled mean benefit. This is a result for the disclosed shortlist, not a proof that every possible investment is unattractive. Inspection may have information value not priced by this model.

## Modeling and intervention assumptions

The weekday demand forecast smooths sparse machine history toward machine and floor weekday averages; the smoothing weight is fixed at 10 observations. May–June wagers and observed uptime are not predictors. Tests alter held-out wagers and verify predictions do not change. July is refit on January–June; no July actual outcomes exist.

July predicted wagers × historical configured hold supply move, lease and signage baselines. Maintenance instead uses historical downtime opportunity across its full eligible scope. Intervention net benefit subtracts catalogue cash costs and planned downtime opportunity, after lead time. A manufacturer maintenance sweep covers the manufacturer group, not one cabinet. The engine shares seeded demand/retention draws to make comparisons repeatable. The demand multiplier spans 0.85–1.15. Probabilities are conditional on these assumptions, not empirical success rates.

The shortlist uses one catalogue unit per candidate, excludes overlapping machine targets, and enforces comp-policy incompatibilities. Purchased machines and whole-bank moves are not in the current shortlist. The keep-budget recommendation does not require spending all $100,000 or choosing three action types.

## How we would know an action worked

| Action | Predefined evidence and comparison | Success decision |
|---|---|---|
| Keep budget / monitor | Track availability, expected earnings per scheduled hour and payout diagnostics; review the same model error measures on genuinely new outcomes. | Reconsider only when new evidence or a supported effect estimate changes the cost-benefit comparison. No claimed cash uplift from doing nothing. |
| Inspection | Check settings, published game math and meter reconciliation; record discrepancies and corrections. | Verified discrepancy resolved. Price any corrective work separately; do not equate a random payout recovery with success. |
| Move / replacement / signage | Record targets, dates and downtime; compare pre/post changes with matched untreated machines or zones. Check total floor play for displacement. | Additional contribution after cash costs and planned downtime exceeds $0, with uncertainty reported; not merely higher treated-machine wagers. |
| Maintenance / staffing | Compare unavailable hours, repeat faults and earnings per scheduled hour against an untreated comparison group. | Evidence of recovered availability and positive incremental contribution after costs, without displaced floor play. |
| Comp pilot | Randomize eligible participants; track economic comp costs, incremental contribution and voluntary non-gaming engagement. Avoid concurrent floor-wide policy change. | Positive contribution after economic costs with uncertainty and guest-protection checks. No targeting of high-loss players. |

These are proposed evaluation plans, not completed experiments. Before implementation, fix the exact comparison group, material effect threshold, sample size and stopping rules. Thirty days may not provide enough power; do not declare success from an inconclusive result.

## Traceability, validation and access

- Raw input hashes are in `data/analysis_results.json` and `data/forecast_metadata.json`; the app rejects mismatched daily-data/forecast exports.
- Reproduce the original exports with `analysis/pipeline.py`, then extensions with `scripts/build_dashboard_data.py`; see README for paths and commands.
- `tests/test_results.py` checks original reconciliations. `tests/test_upgrade.py` checks current formulas, temporal leakage, eligibility and shared scenario consistency. `tests/test_app.py` exercises app controls; passing tests does not establish causality or complete accessibility compliance.
- `notebooks/01_data_checks.ipynb` preserves original Databricks evidence. The current model/map logic is in `notebooks/02_Floor_and_Forecast.ipynb` and shared Python modules. The updated notebook has been exercised locally, not run in the Databricks workspace.
- `analysis_results.json` contains older `forecast` and `simulation` results. The current app uses `forecast_metadata.json`, `demand_predictions.parquet` and `DecisionEngine` instead. Do not mix version-1 forecast numbers with this appendix.
- Sidebar dates update machine/property evidence. Player headline totals/concentration remain full January–June; guest/comp comparisons use labeled complete months. Forecast train/test periods stay fixed.
- This package can be opened locally using `JUDGE_START_HERE.md`. A localhost address is not a public judging link. See the final delivery message for whether online deployment was completed.
